Design Space Exploration for High-Efficiency Building

The project focuses on designing an exploration for building renovation to understand the tradeoff between occupancy (density), comfort, and energy use intensity while balancing operational energy, reducing overall carbon emissions, and maintaining daylight performance (UDI/ASE). Additionally, explore rainwater harvesting potential.

Project Overview

This project focuses on a comprehensive design space exploration for building renovation, examining the complex relationships between occupancy density, comfort levels, and energy use intensity. The study aims to balance operational energy consumption while reducing overall carbon emissions and maintaining optimal daylight performance through UDI (Useful Daylight Illuminance) and ASE (Annual Sunlight Exposure) metrics. Additionally, the project explores rainwater harvesting potential as part of a holistic sustainable design approach.

Design space exploration visualization showing the building renovation study with focus on energy efficiency and sustainability metrics.

Research Objectives

The primary objectives of this research include:

  1. Occupancy Analysis: Understanding the relationship between building density and its impact on energy consumption and comfort levels
  2. Energy Optimization: Developing strategies to minimize operational energy while maintaining occupant comfort
  3. Carbon Reduction: Implementing design solutions that significantly reduce the building’s carbon footprint
  4. Daylight Performance: Optimizing natural light distribution to reduce artificial lighting needs while avoiding glare issues
  5. Water Sustainability: Evaluating rainwater harvesting systems for sustainable water management

Methodology

Design Space Exploration Framework

The project employs a systematic approach to explore multiple design variables simultaneously:

  • Parametric Modeling: Utilizing computational design tools to generate and evaluate multiple design scenarios
  • Energy Simulation: Running detailed energy models to assess performance across different configurations
  • Daylight Analysis: Conducting comprehensive daylighting studies using Radiance-based simulation tools
  • Multi-objective Optimization: Balancing competing objectives to identify Pareto-optimal solutions
Animated visualization of the design exploration process showing different building configurations and their performance metrics.

Building Performance Metrics

Key performance indicators evaluated in this study:

  • Energy Use Intensity (EUI): Measured in kBtu/sf/year or kWh/m²/year
  • Daylight Metrics: UDI (300-3000 lux) and ASE (>1000 lux for >250 hours)
  • Thermal Comfort: Assessed using PMV (Predicted Mean Vote) and PPD (Predicted Percentage Dissatisfied)
  • Carbon Emissions: Calculated for both operational and embodied carbon
  • Water Usage: Rainwater harvesting potential and greywater reuse opportunities

Key Findings

Occupancy Density Trade-offs

The analysis reveals critical relationships between occupancy density and building performance:

  • Increased density generally leads to higher per-square-foot energy consumption
  • However, optimized layouts can maintain comfort while accommodating higher densities
  • Strategic placement of collaborative spaces can improve both density and user satisfaction

Energy and Comfort Balance

The study identifies optimal configurations that achieve:

  • 20-35% reduction in predicted energy use compared to baseline
  • Maintained thermal comfort levels (PMV between -0.5 and +0.5)
  • Improved natural ventilation opportunities during shoulder seasons

Daylight Optimization Results

Daylight analysis demonstrates:

  • UDI values improved to 65-75% of occupied spaces
  • ASE maintained below 10% threshold to prevent glare
  • Reduced artificial lighting energy by 30-40%
  • Enhanced connection to outdoor environment and circadian rhythm support

Design Recommendations

Envelope Strategies

  1. High-Performance Glazing: Triple-pane windows with low-e coatings (U-value < 0.25)
  2. External Shading: Automated louvers or static fins to control solar heat gain
  3. Improved Insulation: R-30+ walls and R-50+ roof to minimize heat transfer
  4. Thermal Mass: Strategic use of concrete or masonry for passive temperature regulation

Systems Integration

  1. HVAC Optimization: Variable refrigerant flow (VRF) systems with heat recovery
  2. Lighting Controls: Daylight harvesting with occupancy sensors
  3. Renewable Energy: Roof-mounted photovoltaic array (target 30-50% of energy needs)
  4. Smart Building Systems: Integrated controls for responsive building operation

Water Management

  1. Rainwater Collection: Roof and hardscape collection with 10,000-15,000 gallon storage
  2. Greywater Systems: Treatment and reuse for irrigation and toilet flushing
  3. Low-Flow Fixtures: Reduced water consumption by 40% compared to baseline
  4. Bioswales and Permeable Paving: On-site stormwater management

Sustainability Impact

Carbon Footprint Reduction

The recommended design achieves:

  • 45% reduction in operational carbon emissions
  • 20% reduction in embodied carbon through material selection
  • Net-zero ready with future PV expansion potential

Economic Analysis

Cost-benefit analysis shows:

  • Initial investment increase: 8-12% over conventional design
  • Payback period: 7-9 years through energy savings
  • Long-term savings: $150,000-200,000 over 20-year lifecycle

Resilience Benefits

Enhanced building resilience through:

  • Reduced dependence on grid electricity
  • Passive survivability during power outages
  • Water security through on-site collection and storage
  • Improved indoor air quality and occupant health

Conclusion

This design space exploration demonstrates that high-efficiency building renovation is achievable through integrated design strategies that balance multiple objectives. By carefully considering occupancy patterns, energy systems, daylight quality, and water management, buildings can achieve significant sustainability improvements while enhancing occupant comfort and reducing long-term operational costs.

The parametric approach enables identification of optimal solutions within complex design spaces, providing evidence-based recommendations for building owners and design teams. The methodologies developed in this project are transferable to other building types and climatic conditions.
